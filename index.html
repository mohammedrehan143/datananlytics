AIML Lab Experiment Exercises & Solutions

Interactive configuration and step-by-step verification

Exercise 1

Interactive Binomial Distribution UI

Parameters: n = 15, p = 0.6

1. Mean and Variance:
Mean = n · p = 15 × 0.6 = 9.0
Variance = n · p · (1 - p) = 15 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 3.6
Std. Dev = √3.6 ≈ 1.8974
2. P(X = 9) — Probability of exactly 9 successes:
P(X = 9) = C(15, 9) · (0.6)9 · (0.4)6 = 0.2066 (20.66%)
3. P(X ≤ 10) — Probability of at most 10 successes:
P(X ≤ 10) = ∑i=0..10 P(X = i) = 0.7827 (78.27%)
Complement P(X > 10) = 0.2173 (21.73%)
4. Region of Highest Probability:
The peak (mode) of the PMF curve is centered at X = 9 (with probability 0.2066), matching the theoretical mean μ = 9.
5. Inference from Visualization:

Because the success probability p = 0.6 > 0.5, the distribution is slightly left-skewed (negatively skewed), with skewness γ1 = (1 - 2(0.6)) / √3.6 = -0.1054. The mass of probability is concentrated towards the upper half of trials (centered around X = 9).

Exercise 2

Interactive Poisson Distribution UI

AI-based Monitoring System: λ = 6 events on average

1. Mean and Variance:
Mean (μ) = λ = 6.0
Variance (σ²) = λ = 6.0
Std. Dev = √6 ≈ 2.4495
2. P(X = 8) — Probability of exactly 8 events:
P(X = 8) = (e-6 · 68) / 8! = 0.1033 (10.33%)
3. P(X ≤ 5) — Probability of at most 5 events:
P(X ≤ 5) = ∑i=0..5 (e-6 · 6i) / i! = 0.4457 (44.57%)
4. Comparison of Poisson PMF for λ = 2, 6, and 10:
  • λ = 2: Highly right-skewed, peak at X=1 and X=2, rapid decay.
  • λ = 6: Moderately skewed, peak at X=5 and X=6, broader spread.
  • λ = 10: Increasingly symmetric, peak at X=9 and X=10, bell-shaped.
5. Inference from Graph:

As λ increases: (i) The central tendency (mean μ = λ) shifts to the right; (ii) The dispersion increases because variance equals λ (σ = √λ); (iii) The skewness decreases (γ1 = 1/√λ → 0), causing the distribution to transform from an asymmetric, right-skewed curve into a symmetric bell curve, illustrating the Normal approximation to the Poisson distribution.
